# ISIATA: The Interactive Album Experience & Membership Ecosystem

## Master Creative, Product, Experience, and Technical Brief

**Project:** ISIATA interactive album-release platform\
**First campaign:** *Feel No Evil*\
**Document purpose:** Master brief for creative development, experience
design, website development, CRM/membership architecture, and
implementation with Claude Code or another coding agent.\
**Status:** Strategic concept and product direction. This document
captures decisions and ideas discussed so far. Items marked **Proposed**
are recommendations, not locked decisions.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 1. Executive Overview

ISIATA is developing a repeatable, interactive digital experience that
turns major music releases into participatory events rather than
conventional album campaigns.

The first campaign will be built around the album **Feel No Evil**.
Instead of asking people simply to pre-save an album or join a mailing
list, the campaign invites them into an unfolding mystery that develops
over approximately eight weeks and culminates in an exclusive cinematic
album premiere.

The audience is invited to register for two things from the beginning:

1.  An exclusive cinematic live album premiere.
2.  An eight-week interactive mystery and experience leading up to that
    premiere.

The premiere is the destination. The mystery is the adventure. The album
is the payoff.

Participants receive weekly cinematic transmissions, challenges, clues,
puzzles, collectible fragments, and evolving access to a digital world.
The eight weekly pieces may combine into a larger image, symbol,
revelation, or meta-puzzle. The campaign should be entertaining first.
It can introduce philosophical, scientific, historical, psychological,
or cultural ideas, but it must not feel like a course or an educational
product. The audience should come for mystery, atmosphere, music,
discovery, and entertainment, then leave thinking about something they
had not considered before.

The experience should also establish a permanent ISIATA membership
identity. Registration creates a member profile and a digital identity
card. The card may eventually be added to Apple Wallet and Google
Wallet, and can provide recognition, access, discounts, rewards, and
continuity across future albums, concerts, merchandise releases, and
digital experiences.

The larger ambition is to create a recognizable ISIATA release format:
each major release has its own mythology, story, puzzles, visual world,
and mechanics, while the audience gradually learns to expect an
immersive, interactive experience whenever ISIATA releases something
significant.

This is not simply an album landing page, email funnel, puzzle campaign,
loyalty scheme, or browser game. It is the beginning of a connected
artist-audience ecosystem.

# 2. Vision and Core Principles

## 2.1 The vision

Create a persistent digital home for the ISIATA audience where music,
storytelling, visual art, interactive mystery, community participation,
live events, collectibles, and membership intersect.

The platform should make people feel that they have entered a world
rather than visited a promotional website.

The experience should: - Give people a compelling reason to register
even if they have never heard of ISIATA. - Make the album itself the
central artistic payoff. - Give people something interesting to do every
week. - Reward curiosity, observation, interpretation, collaboration,
and participation. - Build anticipation and shared memories. - Make
audience participation visibly matter. - Create a lasting relationship
between the audience and the artist. - Carry membership and audience
history from one album campaign to the next. - Be reusable without
making every release feel identical.

## 2.2 The core formula

**The premiere is the destination.**\
**The mystery is the adventure.**\
**The album is the payoff.**\
**The membership is what remains.**

This distinction is important. The audience must know from the beginning
that they are registering for an album premiere. The campaign should not
hide the actual destination behind a vague mystery. The mystery adds
anticipation and depth; it does not replace the promised album
experience.

## 2.3 Design principles

1.  **Entertainment before education.** Ideas can be intellectually
    stimulating, but the experience should never feel like homework or a
    course.
2.  **Music is central.** The music should not be an unrelated reward at
    the end. Audio, rhythm, vocals, samples, sound design, and
    composition can all become part of the interactive language.
3.  **Mystery with clarity.** The atmosphere can be mysterious, but
    users must understand what they are joining, what they can do, and
    what they will receive.
4.  **Participation without punishment.** Missing a week should not
    permanently exclude someone from the story, core premiere, or
    meaningful rewards.
5.  **Every week must feel worthwhile.** Each episode should deliver a
    satisfying experience even if a participant never solves the larger
    puzzle.
6.  **A larger arc should reward attention.** People who connect clues
    across multiple weeks should discover an additional layer.
7.  **Audience agency should be real.** Where possible, audience choices
    and contributions should visibly affect the world or final
    presentation.
8.  **The experience should be accessible.** It should work in a mobile
    browser, avoid unnecessary downloads, and support people with
    different levels of puzzle experience.
9.  **Technology serves the experience.** Do not build a complicated
    game merely because it is technically possible.
10. **The membership outlives the campaign.** The audience relationship
    should continue after the album premiere.

# 3. Brand and Creative Positioning

## 3.1 ISIATA

ISIATA is the artist identity and the umbrella for the wider creative
universe. The project should allow music, visual storytelling,
interactive experiences, technology, and community to coexist without
forcing them into separate identities.

The creative archetype informing the experience is the **Magician**: -
Transformation - Discovery - Possibility - Craft - Mystery -
Revelation - The sense that something hidden can become visible

The tone should be cinematic, intriguing, atmospheric, intelligent, and
emotionally resonant. It should avoid generic marketing language, forced
hype, and obvious gamification.

## 3.2 Recurring format versus individual campaigns

The long-term model should have two layers:

**A. A recurring ISIATA experience format**\
A consistent umbrella identity that audiences recognize across major
releases.

**B. A distinct campaign universe for each release**\
Each album or major project gets its own mythology, visual language,
narrative, puzzle structure, and interactive mechanics.

Possible names discussed for the recurring format include: - The
Ritual - The Unknown - The Revelations - The ISIATA Experience - The
Spellbook

No name has been selected. Do not lock the naming system before the
wider brand architecture is explored.

The *Feel No Evil* campaign may have its own subtitle or chapter
identity, such as **Feel No Evil: The Eight Revelations**, but this is
only a working possibility.

## 3.3 Consistency without repetition

The recurring format should be recognizable through recurring elements
such as: - A member identity and profile. - A personal archive or
collection. - A scheduled episode or transmission. - Interactive
discovery. - A community-wide objective. - A final live or cinematic
event. - A permanent record of participation.

The actual world, characters, story, puzzles, symbols, rewards, and
artistic direction should change with each release.

# 4. First Campaign: FEEL NO EVIL

## 4.1 Campaign concept

*Feel No Evil* is the first major test of the ISIATA interactive album
format.

The title creates a useful thematic foundation around: - What we see. -
What we hear. - What we feel. - What we believe. - What we remember. -
What we choose to ignore. - Whether ignorance is innocence or a
choice. - How perspective affects our understanding of reality.

These are thematic possibilities, not a finalized narrative. The
campaign should find a coherent artistic interpretation rather than
mechanically include every idea.

## 4.2 Campaign duration

Working duration: approximately **six to eight weeks**, with eight
weekly chapters as the preferred structure.

The campaign should culminate in a **30--40-minute cinematic live album
debut**. The exact runtime and show structure can be adjusted during
production.

## 4.3 Registration promise

The registration page should clearly state that signing up provides: -
Access to the exclusive cinematic album premiere. - Entry into the
eight-week interactive mystery. - A personal ISIATA member profile. - A
digital membership identity. - Access to weekly transmissions,
challenges, and unlocks. - A place to collect campaign artifacts and
achievements. - Eligibility for campaign-specific bonus experiences and
rewards.

The user should not have to solve all eight puzzles to qualify for the
core album premiere. The premiere is included with registration from the
beginning.

Completing the mystery may unlock additional content, such as: - Bonus
scenes. - Secret performances. - Alternate versions or exclusive
tracks. - Hidden story material. - Special collectibles. - A post-show
experience. - Additional content in the campaign archive.

Late subscribers should be able to access earlier chapters and complete
the mystery retrospectively. The campaign should not become impossible
to finish because someone discovered it in week five.

# 5. The Weekly Experience

## 5.1 The weekly episode format

Each week should feel like a new chapter in a continuing story.

A proposed episode structure:

### 1. The Transmission

A cinematic introduction, ideally around **three minutes** as an initial
target. It introduces the week's atmosphere, story development,
character, location, or mystery.

The video should be understandable to someone who has never heard of
ISIATA and should not require specialist knowledge.

### 2. The Discovery

The participant enters the digital world and investigates. They may
discover: - A new object or artifact. - A visual clue. - A hidden
message. - A sound or musical fragment. - A character or transmission. -
A change in the environment. - A new section of the larger puzzle.

### 3. The Challenge

A riddle, mystery, observation task, audio puzzle, visual puzzle,
sequence challenge, critical-thinking exercise, or collaborative
challenge.

The challenge should be fun and satisfying. It should not rely on
obscure trivia or knowledge that the audience could not reasonably
discover.

### 4. The Revelation

The participant receives a satisfying resolution: - The answer or
solution. - A short explanation of how the clue worked. - The underlying
idea or concept, if relevant. - A new collectible or artifact. - A
visible change in the world. - A clue that points toward the next
chapter.

Scientific, historical, and philosophical concepts should be represented
accurately. Clearly distinguish factual explanation from fictional
campaign lore.

### 5. The Continuation

The episode ends by introducing a new question, image, sound, object, or
unresolved detail that creates anticipation for the next week.

## 5.2 Weekly appointment

A recurring scheduled premiere time could create a ritual around the
campaign.

A possible weekly live format: - 3-minute cinematic opening. -
Introduction of the week's mystery. - Live exploration or clue
presentation. - Audience investigation. - Community theories or selected
submissions. - A collective decision or progress event where
appropriate. - The reveal. - A teaser for the next chapter.

A live session of roughly 15--20 minutes could be tested, but the exact
length is not decided.

The experience should remain available afterward. Real-time attendance
may provide unique shared moments, live reactions, limited-time
discoveries, or influence over a decision, but people who cannot attend
should still be able to participate.

## 5.3 The eight-chapter thematic arc

The following is a possible starting structure. It is not finalized.

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  Week                    Theme                   Possible mechanic
  ----------------------- ----------------------- -----------------------
  1                       Perception              Optical illusion or
                                                  visual observation

  2                       Truth                   Conflicting evidence or
                                                  unreliable accounts

  3                       Memory                  Reconstruct a sequence
                                                  or altered memory

  4                       Sound                   Decode an audio message

  5                       Ignorance               Identify what is
                                                  missing or deliberately
                                                  hidden

  6                       Perspective             Visual paradox or
                                                  alternate viewpoints

  7                       Awareness               Connect discoveries
                                                  from earlier chapters

  8                       Revelation              Solve the larger
                                                  mystery and prepare for
                                                  the premiere
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------

Each week should have its own satisfying challenge and contribute
something to the larger arc.

## 5.4 The eight-piece meta-puzzle

Each weekly chapter can award a fragment: - A piece of an image. - A
symbol. - A word. - A sound. - A map coordinate. - A visual pattern. - A
section of a larger message.

Across eight weeks, the fragments combine into a larger revelation.

The complete image, phrase, symbol, or key should mean something in the
context of *Feel No Evil*. It should not feel like eight unrelated
pieces assembled only to create a reward.

The meta-puzzle should reward people who pay attention over time.
However, each weekly challenge must remain independently enjoyable.

# 6. The Digital World

## 6.1 Core direction

The preferred direction is a persistent, browser-based interactive
environment rather than a conventional campaign landing page.

The user should enter a world that evolves over time. When they return
each week, they should recognize the environment but discover that
something has changed.

The experience should be visually distinctive and atmospheric. It may
use 2D, layered environments, interactive scenes, or selected 3D
elements. The first version does not need to be a fully multiplayer
game.

## 6.2 Possible central environments

Several possible central environments have been discussed: - **The
Sanctuary:** A mysterious central location containing a living tree and
eight artifacts. - **The Observatory:** A place for observing hidden
patterns, signals, stars, and transmissions. - **The Mansion:** A larger
environment with rooms, objects, and hidden stories. - **The
Otherworld:** A surreal environment that can change dramatically between
chapters.

No central environment has been selected.

The environment should be chosen based on the actual story and the
emotional atmosphere of *Feel No Evil*, not just technical convenience.

## 6.3 Persistent world and weekly changes

The world may contain: - A central hub. - Eight objects, rooms,
artifacts, or locations. - Locked and unlocked areas. - Interactive
objects. - Audio and video transmissions. - Environmental changes. -
Hidden details. - A central visual structure that evolves as the
community progresses.

For example, if the Sanctuary and living tree concept is selected, each
week may reveal a new artifact or cause a new part of the tree to
awaken. This is illustrative, not a confirmed design.

Interactions could trigger: - Animations. - Short cinematic sequences. -
Audio messages. - A puzzle interface. - A collectible. - A new
environmental state. - A new route or room.

## 6.4 The Grimoire

A personal collection called **The Grimoire** is a proposed feature.

It would act as a user's private record of their journey through the
ISIATA experience.

Potential contents: - Collected artifacts. - Symbols and fragments. -
Audio transmissions. - Solved riddles. - Achievements. - Campaign
history. - Member identity. - Premiere pass. - Special unlocks. - Past
campaign records.

The Grimoire can become a recurring feature across future releases, with
each campaign adding a new section.

It should be easy to understand and use on mobile. It should not become
a complicated inventory-management system.

# 7. Participation, Engagement, and Community Mechanics

The primary engagement goal is not simply to collect registrations or
generate website visits. It is to make people want to return every week.

The key measure is **weekly active participation and retention**.

## 7.1 Core mechanics to prioritize

The first version should prioritize a small number of high-quality
mechanics:

1.  Weekly cinematic episodes.
2.  Hidden clues and riddles.
3.  Collectibles and achievements.
4.  A community-wide objective or shared progress system.

These mechanics create a loop of anticipation, discovery, progress, and
return.

## 7.2 Additional mechanics to consider

The following are potential enhancements. They should not all be built
at once.

### Hidden secrets

Unannounced clues can appear in: - Episode backgrounds. - Album
artwork. - Music videos. - Reversed vocals. - Audio stems or mixes. -
Website details. - Email content. - Previously unlocked environments.

These should reward close observation without making the main experience
inaccessible.

### Global community challenge

All participants contribute to a shared objective.

For example, solving challenges, collecting fragments, or submitting
discoveries may advance a shared progress meter. When the community
reaches 100%, a bonus cinematic sequence or collective unlock becomes
available to everyone.

The global challenge should reward participation and collaboration, not
simply the fastest individuals.

### Factions or orders

Possible groups discussed: - Seekers. - Keepers. - Forgers. - Watchers.

Factions could create identity, friendly rivalry, shared objectives, and
collaborative problem-solving.

However, factions should be introduced only after the basic experience
has proven that people return. Avoid creating a permanent winner/loser
structure that makes new members feel disadvantaged.

### Audience voting

Audience choices could influence: - A later visual environment. - Which
character or location is explored. - The order of certain scenes. - An
alternate version of a visual. - A bonus performance or post-show
reveal.

The choices must have genuine consequences. Do not ask the audience to
vote on things that have already been decided.

### Audience contribution

Participants may submit: - Words. - Symbols. - Interpretations. - Short
audio recordings. - Sounds. - Images or visual contributions. -
Responses to a weekly question.

Selected contributions could appear in the final premiere, a collective
artwork, an audio composition, an installation, or the campaign archive.

This is a powerful way to make the audience feel that they helped create
something.

### Community theory board

A place for people to discuss clues, share theories, and collaborate.

This may be a built-in discussion area or an integration with an
existing community platform. Moderation, privacy, and abuse prevention
must be considered.

### Shareable achievements

Create attractive, campaign-specific cards or images that users can
share to social platforms when they: - Join the experience. - Complete a
chapter. - Discover a hidden clue. - Complete the meta-puzzle. - Reach a
milestone. - Attend the premiere.

These should create curiosity among people who have not joined. They
should not reveal spoilers by default.

### Surprise events

Occasional unannounced changes can happen between weekly episodes: - An
artifact changes. - A new message appears. - A hidden room opens. - A
character sends a transmission. - A previously seen object reveals a new
clue.

Use these sparingly. Constant surprise notifications can create fatigue.

### Live host or fictional character

A character or artist persona could introduce transmissions, provide
context, respond to discoveries, and guide participants through the
world.

This could be a mysterious narrator, an in-world figure, or a version of
the artist integrated into the mythology.

# 8. Music as an Interactive Mechanic

The music must be integral to the experience. The album should not feel
like an unrelated product attached to a puzzle game.

Possible music-based mechanics: - Reversed vocal phrases. - Hidden
messages in audio. - Stereo-position clues. - Rhythmic patterns. -
Melodic sequences. - Audio-spectrum shapes. - Musical fragments that
combine into a larger composition. - A sound that changes when played in
a particular order. - A clue hidden in an instrumental or alternate
mix. - A recurring motif that gains meaning across chapters.

The puzzles should be solvable by listening and experimenting, not by
owning expensive audio equipment or having professional production
knowledge.

If a clue requires headphones, tell participants. If a clue depends on a
particular audio detail, provide an accessible alternative where
possible.

Music can be used as: 1. Atmosphere. 2. Storytelling. 3. Evidence. 4. A
puzzle. 5. A collectible. 6. A reward. 7. The final artistic payoff.

This is a major differentiator because the artist's actual creative work
becomes the language of the experience.

# 9. The Live Album Premiere

## 9.1 Purpose

The premiere is the campaign's central destination and artistic payoff.

Working target: approximately **30--40 minutes**.

The experience is inspired in part by the scale and event-like quality
of virtual music experiences such as the Travis Scott Fortnite concert,
while remaining its own artistic format.

## 9.2 Possible components

The premiere could combine: - A short film. - Cinematic visual
storytelling. - Music and performance. - A designed digital
environment. - Audience participation. - Previously unlocked
artifacts. - Audience-created material. - A final revelation. - Bonus
scenes or post-show access.

The show should feel like a coherent artistic work, not a sequence of
unrelated promotional videos.

## 9.3 Access rules

Every registered participant receives access to the core premiere.

The eight-week mystery is not a gate that prevents someone from seeing
the album debut.

Puzzle completion can unlock bonus content, secret scenes, bonus
performances, alternate versions, or a special post-show experience.

## 9.4 Technical reliability

The premiere needs to work reliably at the expected audience size.

Keep the live-streaming or synchronized video delivery system separate
from the interactive world where practical.

A pre-rendered cinematic sequence with coordinated audience interactions
may be more reliable than attempting to render a complex, real-time
multiplayer concert for every user.

A dependable fallback is essential. If the interactive world has
problems, users should still be able to access the premiere.

# 10. The ISIATA Digital Membership Ecosystem

## 10.1 Permanent membership identity

A major strategic decision has emerged: the ISIATA membership should be
**permanent**, not a temporary card that expires after one album
campaign.

A person should be able to join during *Feel No Evil* and retain their
identity through future album releases, concerts, merchandise drops, and
experiences.

The card becomes a continuing symbol of their relationship with ISIATA.

The membership should ideally be free to join. Premium products and
experiences can be offered separately, but the basic identity should
remain accessible.

## 10.2 Digital identity card

Every registered member receives a personalized digital ISIATA identity
card.

Possible card details: - ISIATA branding. - Member name or display
name. - Unique member ID. - Membership status. - Founding-member status,
if applicable. - Join date or first campaign. - Current recognition
level. - Optional profile image or avatar. - QR code or secure pass
identifier.

The visual design should be premium, mysterious, collectible, and
consistent with the ISIATA brand.

The identity card should not expose sensitive personal information. A
public-facing QR code should resolve to a safe verification or
membership view, not reveal a user's email, phone number, or private
account data.

## 10.3 Apple Wallet and Google Wallet

The digital card should be designed with the possibility of being added
to: - Apple Wallet. - Google Wallet.

This would make membership tangible and convenient. A member could open
their wallet pass to show their status, access an offer, or verify
eligibility at a concert or merchandise booth.

Wallet support is a product requirement to investigate during technical
planning. It requires the appropriate pass creation, signing, update,
and distribution infrastructure. Do not assume that a website image of a
card is equivalent to a functional Wallet pass.

Potential dynamic pass updates: - Membership status changes. - New
campaign access. - New achievement or recognition. - Updated member
benefits. - Event-specific information.

## 10.4 Membership benefits

Potential benefits include:

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  Area                                Possible benefit
  ----------------------------------- -----------------------------------
  Concerts                            Member discounts, presales, early
                                      access, selected VIP upgrades

  Merchandise                         Member pricing, exclusive items,
                                      early drops

  Music                               Early listening, bonus versions,
                                      exclusive releases

  Digital experiences                 Access to album worlds,
                                      transmissions, archives

  Events                              Member-only digital or physical
                                      experiences

  Recognition                         Achievements, milestones, founding
                                      status

  Community                           Participation in selected decisions
                                      and collective experiences
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------

Benefits should be specific, understandable, and sustainable. Do not
promise a discount or access benefit until the operational process is in
place.

## 10.5 Founding Member status

The first campaign creates an opportunity to recognize the first
generation of members.

A possible permanent designation:

**FOUNDING MEMBER · EST. 2026**

This status could be earned by joining during the first ISIATA
membership campaign, subject to clearly defined eligibility rules.

The emotional value is that members were present before the community
had a history. This status should not be casually recreated or sold
later.

## 10.6 Recognition levels

Possible recognition levels discussed:

-   **Founding Member:** Joined during the first ISIATA experience.
-   **Initiate:** Participated in an album experience.
-   **Insider:** Regularly participates in releases and events.
-   **Inner Circle:** Earned through meaningful participation and
    loyalty.

These names and rules are not finalized.

Recognition should not be purely pay-to-win. Higher recognition should
reflect genuine participation, loyalty, or contribution. Purchases may
unlock product benefits, but should not automatically manufacture
community status.

## 10.7 The member journey

A possible journey:

1.  Person discovers an ISIATA campaign.
2.  Person registers for the album premiere and interactive mystery.
3.  A permanent member profile is created.
4.  A unique member ID is assigned.
5.  The digital identity card becomes available.
6.  The person enters the campaign world.
7.  Weekly participation updates their profile and Grimoire.
8.  Achievements and campaign history accumulate.
9.  The person attends the premiere.
10. The member remains part of the ISIATA ecosystem after the campaign.
11. Future releases and events provide new reasons to return.

# 11. CRM, Member Database, and Data Architecture

## 11.1 Separate the relationship from the access authority

The CRM should manage communication and marketing relationships. A
dedicated membership database should remain the authoritative source for
identity, membership status, entitlements, achievements, and
participation.

Do not make a marketing automation platform the only source of truth for
who is entitled to access a digital experience or discount.

## 11.2 Proposed system responsibilities

### Supabase / PostgreSQL

Potential responsibilities: - Member accounts. - Unique member IDs. -
Authentication and account identity. - Membership status. - Member
profile data. - Campaign registration. - Chapter progress. - Puzzle
completion. - Collectibles. - Achievements. - Grimoire records. -
Community contribution records. - Entitlements and access permissions. -
Wallet pass references and status. - Audit history for important
changes.

### GoHighLevel

Potential responsibilities: - CRM contact records. - Email campaigns. -
SMS campaigns, where users have consented. - Registration
confirmation. - Weekly episode reminders. - Abandoned-registration
follow-up, where appropriate. - Premiere reminders. - Post-premiere
follow-up. - Merchandise and event campaign segmentation. - Member
reactivation. - Marketing analytics.

### ISIATA website and application

Potential responsibilities: - Public campaign pages. - Registration. -
Login and account experience. - Member dashboard. - Digital identity
card display. - Interactive campaign world. - Grimoire. - Weekly
transmissions. - Challenges and puzzles. - Achievement display. -
Premiere access. - Member benefits and offer redemption.

### Apple Wallet / Google Wallet

Potential responsibilities: - Portable digital membership pass. - Member
ID presentation. - QR or barcode-based verification. - Dynamic updates
where supported.

### Media delivery and streaming

Potential responsibilities: - Hosting cinematic videos. - Hosting
audio. - Delivering campaign assets. - Reliable synchronized or live
premiere playback.

The exact vendors and services are not selected.

## 11.3 Data synchronization

A registration or membership event should create or update the member
record in the membership database and synchronize the appropriate
contact fields to the CRM.

The database should determine membership and entitlements. The CRM
should receive the data needed for relevant communications and
segmentation.

Avoid uncontrolled two-way synchronization that can overwrite
authoritative member data.

## 11.4 Suggested member data model

A starting conceptual schema:

**members** - id - member_number - auth_user_id - display_name - email -
email_consent - sms_consent - created_at - membership_status -
founding_member_status - recognition_level - wallet_pass_status

**campaigns** - id - campaign_slug - campaign_name - release_name -
start_at - end_at - campaign_status

**campaign_members** - id - member_id - campaign_id - registration_at -
premiere_access_status - campaign_completion_status

**chapters** - id - campaign_id - chapter_number - title - release_at -
content_status

**chapter_progress** - id - member_id - chapter_id - viewed_at -
challenge_started_at - completed_at - completion_status

**puzzle_submissions** - id - member_id - chapter_id - submission_data -
result - submitted_at

**artifacts** - id - campaign_id - artifact_name - artifact_type -
asset_reference - unlock_rule

**member_artifacts** - id - member_id - artifact_id - unlocked_at

**achievements** - id - achievement_name - description - campaign_id -
unlock_rule

**member_achievements** - id - member_id - achievement_id - awarded_at

**entitlements** - id - entitlement_name - entitlement_type -
campaign_id - eligibility_rule - valid_from - valid_until

**member_entitlements** - id - member_id - entitlement_id - status -
issued_at - redeemed_at

**community_progress** - id - campaign_id - objective_id -
progress_value - updated_at

**contributions** - id - member_id - campaign_id - contribution_type -
content_reference - moderation_status - submitted_at

**wallet_passes** - id - member_id - platform - pass_reference -
status - last_updated_at

This is a conceptual starting point, not a final production schema. The
implementation should include foreign keys, indexes, uniqueness
constraints, timestamps, validation, access policies, and migration
strategy.

## 11.5 Privacy and consent

The experience will collect personal information and participation data.
Build privacy into the product from the beginning.

Requirements: - Collect only information that is needed. - Explain what
registration data is used for. - Separate account creation from optional
marketing consent. - Make email and SMS consent explicit and
recordable. - Provide unsubscribe and account-management options. - Do
not expose private member information through public profile or QR
code. - Restrict database access using appropriate server-side
authorization and row-level security. - Do not put privileged service
keys in browser code. - Validate and rate-limit submissions. - Moderate
user-generated content before public display. - Establish retention and
deletion processes. - Consider applicable privacy obligations in the
markets where the service operates.

# 12. Website and Application Experience

The website is the front door to the ISIATA universe. It should support
both public discovery and authenticated member experiences.

## 12.1 Public-facing pages

Potential pages: 1. Campaign landing page. 2. About the experience. 3.
Registration. 4. Premiere information. 5. Login. 6. Privacy policy and
terms. 7. Help / FAQ.

The landing page should communicate the promise clearly: - This is an
album premiere. - There is an eight-week interactive mystery. -
Registration is free, if that remains the chosen model. - The experience
has weekly chapters. - The audience can participate at their own pace. -
The core premiere is included with registration.

## 12.2 Authenticated member area

Potential sections: - Member home. - Digital identity card. - Current
campaign. - Interactive world. - Weekly episodes. - Challenge
interface. - The Grimoire. - Achievements. - Premiere access. - Member
benefits. - Account and privacy settings.

## 12.3 Mobile-first requirement

A significant portion of the audience will likely enter through social
media links on a phone.

The experience must: - Work well on mobile browsers. - Load quickly on
mobile connections. - Have readable typography. - Use large, clear
interactive targets. - Avoid requiring a desktop for core puzzles. -
Provide accessible alternatives for complex audio or visual
interactions. - Preserve progress across devices. - Handle interrupted
sessions gracefully.

Desktop can provide a richer visual experience, but mobile must not be a
second-class version.

## 12.4 Performance and resilience

The website should not require a high-end gaming computer.

Use optimized media, compressed assets, lazy loading, sensible caching,
and progressive enhancement. Provide a simplified experience when 3D
rendering is unavailable or a device is underpowered.

The core functions---registration, member account, campaign access,
puzzle completion, and premiere access---must not depend on an elaborate
visual renderer working perfectly.

# 13. Technical Direction

## 13.1 Proposed starting stack

The following stack has been discussed as a reasonable direction,
subject to validation:

-   **Next.js:** Website and application framework.
-   **React:** User interface.
-   **Three.js / React Three Fiber:** Interactive 3D environments where
    useful.
-   **Supabase:** Authentication, PostgreSQL database, storage, and
    real-time features where appropriate.
-   **GoHighLevel:** CRM and marketing automation.
-   **CDN / media delivery provider:** Video, audio, and image delivery.
-   **Apple Wallet / Google Wallet pass infrastructure:** Digital
    membership cards.
-   **Streaming provider:** Reliable premiere delivery.

No final hosting provider, streaming provider, or Wallet pass vendor has
been selected.

## 13.2 Do not start with a full multiplayer game

A fully multiplayer 3D environment would add substantial complexity: -
Real-time synchronization. - Concurrent user management. - Server-side
state. - Rendering and performance constraints. - Moderation and
safety. - Hosting costs. - Device compatibility. - Testing complexity.

The first version should create the *feeling* of entering a living world
without requiring a fully shared real-time game.

A user-specific interactive environment with shared community progress
can provide much of the desired experience.

## 13.3 Suggested progressive implementation

**Stage 1: Functional foundation** - Public landing page. - Registration
and authentication. - Member record creation. - Member dashboard. -
Basic identity card. - Campaign and chapter data. - Email/CRM
synchronization.

**Stage 2: Interactive campaign** - Central world or hub. - Weekly
chapter content. - Interactive objects. - Puzzle submission and
validation. - Chapter progress. - Collectibles. - Grimoire.

**Stage 3: Community systems** - Shared progress meter. - Community
contributions. - Voting. - Theory/discussion functionality, if
justified. - Achievement sharing.

**Stage 4: Membership infrastructure** - Permanent member identity. -
Member benefits. - Offer and discount validation. - Wallet pass
generation and updates. - Member recognition levels.

**Stage 5: Premiere** - Reliable video or live-stream delivery. -
Premiere access control. - Event-specific interactions. - Bonus content
unlocks. - Fallback playback route.

**Stage 6: Post-campaign and future campaigns** - Campaign archive. -
Replay and solutions. - Member history. - New campaign creation
workflow. - Reusable campaign components.

## 13.4 Reliability fallback

The experience should have a fallback mode.

If the 3D environment fails, participants should still be able to: - Log
in. - View the episode. - Read or hear the clues. - Submit answers. -
Access their Grimoire. - Attend the premiere.

The visual world enhances the experience; it should not become a single
point of failure.

# 16. Accessibility, Fairness, and Moderation

The experience should be challenging without being exclusionary.

Consider: - Hints for participants who get stuck. - Multiple difficulty
levels or optional bonus challenges. - Clear explanations after
reveals. - Retrospective access to earlier chapters. - No permanent loss
of core content for missing a scheduled event. - Audio alternatives
where a puzzle depends on visual perception. - Visual alternatives where
a puzzle depends on audio. - Captions and transcripts for cinematic
transmissions. - Mobile-friendly interactions. - No requirement to
purchase products to solve the main mystery. - Clear rules for voting
and community challenges. - Moderation for public submissions and
discussions. - Protection against answer brute-forcing, spam, and
automated abuse.

The main story should be accessible to a broad audience. Deep secrets
and bonus discoveries can provide extra complexity for highly engaged
participants.

# 18. Risks and Things Not to Overbuild

The creative ambition is large. The biggest danger is building too much
before validating the core experience.

## Main risks

1.  **Over-engineering:** A complex world can consume the budget and
    delay the campaign without increasing participation.
2.  **Unclear onboarding:** People may be intrigued but not understand
    what to do.
3.  **Puzzle frustration:** Challenges that are too obscure will make
    people leave.
4.  **Weak weekly payoff:** If an episode does not deliver something
    satisfying, people may stop returning.
5.  **Premiere reliability:** A technical failure at the main event
    would damage trust.
6.  **Too many mechanics:** Factions, voting, collectibles, live chat,
    hidden events, and community submissions can overwhelm the first
    version.
7.  **Membership without value:** A card alone does not create loyalty.
    The benefits and ongoing relationship must justify its existence.
8.  **Reward inflation:** If every interaction gives an arbitrary badge,
    achievements lose meaning.
9.  **Privacy and security:** A CRM-connected membership system needs
    clear consent and access controls.
10. **Production workload:** Eight cinematic episodes plus puzzles,
    environment design, music, and a finale is a serious production
    commitment.

## Recommended discipline

Build a small number of excellent interactions first.

The first version should prove: - People understand the promise. -
People register. - People enjoy the first chapter. - People return for
the next chapter. - The puzzle mechanics work. - The world performs on
mobile. - The member identity is useful and appealing. - The premiere
can be delivered reliably.

Only then expand into more complex mechanics.

# 21. Guidance for the Coding Agent

This document is the product and creative brief. It is not permission to
invent every unresolved creative decision or immediately build the
entire system.

The coding agent should work in deliberate stages.

## 21.1 First assignment

Start by: 1. Reading this entire brief. 2. Extracting the confirmed
directions, proposed ideas, and unresolved questions. 3. Proposing a
clear product architecture. 4. Proposing the initial application
structure. 5. Identifying technical dependencies and risks. 6.
Identifying decisions that require creative approval. 7. Producing an
implementation plan with milestones. 8. Recommending a minimum viable
first release.

Do not silently decide the campaign's story, visual mythology, puzzle
answers, membership rules, or commercial benefits. Surface those
decisions for approval.

## 21.2 Engineering principles

-   Build reusable campaign components rather than hardcoding the entire
    product around *Feel No Evil*.
-   Treat campaigns, chapters, artifacts, puzzles, rewards, and unlock
    rules as data wherever practical.
-   Keep membership identity separate from campaign-specific progress.
-   Keep the database authoritative for permissions and entitlements.
-   Use server-side validation for important actions.
-   Use secure authentication and authorization.
-   Apply database row-level security appropriately.
-   Never expose secret keys in client-side code.
-   Make important state changes auditable.
-   Design responsive layouts from the beginning.
-   Provide loading, empty, error, and fallback states.
-   Make the core experience usable without 3D.
-   Optimize media and assets.
-   Add automated tests for authentication, access control, puzzle
    submissions, progression, and entitlements.
-   Document setup, environment variables, deployment, migrations, and
    operational procedures.
-   Do not introduce unnecessary infrastructure before the product needs
    it.

## 21.3 Architecture should support future campaigns

The system should not be a one-off *Feel No Evil* website.

A future campaign should be able to introduce: - A new title and visual
identity. - A new world. - New chapter content. - New puzzles. - New
artifacts. - New achievements. - New community objectives. - A new
premiere. - New rewards.

The permanent member account, identity card, and historical Grimoire
should persist.

## 21.4 Suggested first technical milestone

Before building the full 3D world, create a working vertical slice:

1.  A visitor registers.
2.  A member account is created.
3.  A unique member ID is assigned.
4.  The member sees their identity card.
5.  The member enters the campaign.
6.  The member opens the first transmission.
7.  The member interacts with one object.
8.  The member solves one sample challenge.
9.  The member receives one artifact.
10. The artifact appears in their Grimoire.
11. Their progress is saved and persists after logout and login.
12. The relevant CRM contact is created or updated.

This proves the core product architecture before significant investment
in the visual world.

# 22. Final Product Statement

The goal is to build a permanent interactive home for the ISIATA
audience, beginning with the *Feel No Evil* album experience.

A person should be able to discover an ISIATA release, enter its world,
experience its story, solve mysteries, discover music, collect
artifacts, participate with other people, attend a cinematic premiere,
and retain a permanent membership identity after the campaign ends.

Each major release becomes a new chapter in the wider ISIATA universe.

The music changes. The world changes. The mystery changes. The member
remains.

**This is the transition from releasing music to creating an experience
people can enter, participate in, remember, and return to.**
